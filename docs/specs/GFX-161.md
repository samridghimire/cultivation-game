# GFX-161: Fight and encounter backdrops that know the season

Role: world-ui. Reference: Wandering Sword's battle backdrops and Stardew Valley's seasonal palettes (look only, never their art).

## Problem (planner shots 2026-11-15 18:10, `--season=Winter --region=fallen_star_market`)
- combat_report.png: a winter-market fight stands on bright summer-green grass under grey roof silhouettes. The map behind the
  card is snow; the backdrop is summer.
- encounter.png (same day): the strip's ground is summer green too, and the woods backdrop is still one row of near-identical
  grey triangles on one baseline (GFX-157 Follow-up: "encounter-panel pines still read fairly uniform at small scale").

## Where
- `src/ui/duel_stage.gd`: `_draw_ground()` (~line 600) builds the ground strip with
  `GroundTiles.ground_texture(base, rid, size, false)`: no season is passed, so it is always the summer texture.
  `_draw_backdrop()` (~406) draws sky (already seasonal via `sky_colors`), far ridge, near silhouettes (`pines`, `roofs`,
  `reeds`, crags, hills) and props.
- `src/ui/encounter_scene.gd` extends DuelStage, so both get the fix. The season card (WU-501, `season_look`) already
  overrides ground and sky: keep that override working unchanged.
- `GroundTiles.ground_season(season, map)` (ground_tiles.gd ~449) maps the calendar season to the ground season and returns ""
  for `no_snow` regions in winter. Mind the casing: `sky_colors` takes `Calendar.season_of()`'s value with lowercase keys,
  GroundTiles uses "Winter"/"Autumn"/"Spring". Check `Calendar.season_of` and convert once in a small static helper.

## Do
1. **Seasonal ground.** In `_draw_ground()`, pass the season (through `GroundTiles.ground_season`) to `ground_texture`, unless
   `season_look` has a ground (the season card). Tufts: in winter draw them as sparse 1-2 px dark stalks poking through
   (base darkened), not lightened green; in autumn tint them toward ochre.
2. **Seasonal silhouettes.** Add `static func season_tint(season: String) -> Dictionary` (pure, tested) returning how the near
   and far layers change: winter = far ridge lerped 35% toward the snow sky colour and every near piece gets a snow cap (pines: a
   1-2 px pale band on the top edge of each tier; roofs: a pale strip along the ridge and eaves; crags/hills: a pale cap on
   the top ~20% of the shape); autumn = hill/"hills" near pieces lerped 25% toward a muted ochre (pines stay evergreen);
   spring/summer = unchanged. Draw the caps in `_draw_backdrop` after each piece. No snow caps in `no_snow` regions.
3. **Pines with depth (GFX-157 Follow-up).** In `silhouette_shapes(..., "pines", ...)`: two rows. A back row (every piece
   flagged `back`: 55-75% height, lerped 30% toward the haze, baseline 4-6 px higher) and the existing front row, where about
   1 in 4 positions is skipped (a clearing, by `unit_hash`), about 1 in 5 is a broadleaf crown (a round 2-3 circle canopy on
   a trunk, flagged `broad`) and the `dark` pieces stay. Height range 50-150%. Keep it deterministic per region id and keep
   the piece count bounded (the `k < 80` guard).
4. Keep everything deterministic (`unit_hash`, no rng, no `randf`). Never put a prop or silhouette centre in a fighter's foot
   zone (the existing PROP_ZONES rule).

## Tests (tests/unit, extend the DuelStage/EncounterScene tests that exist; grep `silhouette_shapes`)
- `season_tint("Winter")` has a snow cap flag and a far lerp > 0; `season_tint("Summer")` changes nothing.
- The pine silhouette list for a fixed region id contains at least one `back`, one `broad` and has fewer front pieces than
  positions walked (a clearing), and is identical on two calls.
- The helper that converts `Calendar.season_of` to the ground season returns "" for a `no_snow` map in winter.
